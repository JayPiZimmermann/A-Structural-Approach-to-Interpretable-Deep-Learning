For f'=d and a<b, the closed profile P is constant f(a)-d(a)*a on the
left, f(z)-d(a)*z in the central interval, and the affine continuation
with slope d(b)-d(a) on the right. Q=P-f. The proof glues values and first
derivatives at both thresholds. With d decreasing on both tails and
increasing centrally, P and Q are convex. When d<=0 on the left tail and
d>=0 on the right tail, both profile derivatives belong to [0,d(b)-d(a)].
The asymmetric lifts are therefore convex and coordinatewise monotone on
the whole real plane, and their difference equals f(x-y).

The hypotheses describe the actual source derivative, not the constructed
functions' final convexity or monotonicity. When a<=0<=b, P(0)=f(0) and
Q(0)=0; nonnegative-quadrant output preservation would additionally need
f(0)>=0, which this generic theorem does not assume or silently claim.
