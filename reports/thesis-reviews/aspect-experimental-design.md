# Review of experimental design and evidential scope

This review assesses the experimental argument of *A Structural Approach to Interpretable Deep Learning*: whether the research questions, sampling, selection procedures, comparisons, and conclusions fit together. It applies the [master's thesis assessment framework](assessment-criteria.md), rather than conference acceptance criteria. The empirical chapters integrate a CVPR 2026 Highlight paper and a preprint. That is substantial research achievement for a master's thesis, but the publication status does not settle the quality of the integrated experimental argument.

The evidence supports a strong, technically ambitious thesis with unusually extensive evaluation. It does **not yet make a fully persuasive 1.0 case on experimental design alone**: the benchmark population is incompletely described, the implications of unequal tuning opportunities are underexamined, and some interpretations exceed what the comparisons identify. These problems are mostly repairable through precise documentation, narrower claims, and limited analyses of existing results. They do not justify demanding an entirely new benchmark campaign.

## What the design does well

The game campaign has a genuine evaluation protocol, not just an assortment of favorable heatmaps. It declares a 50-image validation set and a separate 565-image test set; selects localization and faithfulness configurations independently; tunes several strong baselines; specifies tie-breaking and inadmissibility rules; and reports paired uncertainty and corrected comparisons. Its one-factor test ablations are explicitly diagnostic and do not feed back into selection. These are material strengths. The thesis also reports that better localization can cost faithfulness and robustness, rather than presenting every metric as a victory. See [the main protocol and results](../../tex/chapters/08_experiments.tex#L75) and [selection and statistics](../../tex/chapters/10_appendix.tex#L275).

The Pascal VOC test is particularly useful: the game settings are frozen from ImageNet-S while baselines may retune on VOC. Images, rather than individual image–class targets, determine the validation/test partition. The thesis distinguishes 2,743 test targets from 2,293 test images. These choices deserve credit; there is no basis here for alleging same-image leakage across the two partitions. The thesis also explicitly separates the two papers' Pixel Flipping definitions, perturbation budgets, and architectures, avoiding a misleading numerical league table across incompatible campaigns ([campaign distinctions](../../tex/chapters/08_experiments.tex#L7), [VOC protocol](../../tex/chapters/10_appendix.tex#L439)).

## The benchmark population needs a complete account

**Confirmed documentation defect.** The appendix describes a custom 50-class ImageNet-S subset “sampled once” and explains the removal of one test image whose mask disappears under cropping. The released game dataset implementation gives a materially more specific construction: 752 masks, 136 exclusions where ImageNet-S and official ILSVRC labels disagree, a 616-image eligible pool, 50 validation images, 566 initial test images, and 565 crop-visible test images. The filter is implemented, not merely mentioned in a comment ([thesis description](../../tex/chapters/10_appendix.tex#L73); [dataset explanation](../../code/playing_backward/data/imagenet_s.py#L63); [filter](../../code/playing_backward/data/imagenet_s.py#L137)).

This matters because the experiment concerns a label-agreeing, crop-visible population, rather than an unspecified random sample of ImageNet-S. The filter has a reasonable purpose: make the explained ImageNet target agree with the segmented object. Nothing in this inspection establishes cherry-picking. Nevertheless, readers should not have to reverse-engineer the sampling population from source code. Add a compact cohort flow, the eligibility rationale, per-class counts, and a link to the exact split identifiers. Explain separately whether the same filtering procedure generated the older split-method campaign; matching sample counts alone cannot establish that.

## Selection and comparison need more critical interpretation

**Documented design limitation, not demonstrated leakage.** Selecting among 672 ViT RG configurations, or 544 CNN RG configurations, using one validation image per class is a fragile basis for identifying a uniquely preferable parameter setting. The subsequent held-out test still supplies legitimate evidence for the selected configuration. Its per-image confidence intervals, however, do not describe how the selected configuration would change under a different validation sample ([declared grids](../../tex/chapters/10_appendix.tex#L210)).

A proportionate improvement is a validation-only selection-stability analysis: use the existing per-image grid outputs to report selection frequencies and near-optimal parameter regions under leave-class-out or clearly defined resampling. Do not call this an independent test replication or use it to choose a new winner after examining test outcomes. If those artifacts are unavailable, state that selection stability remains unmeasured and describe conclusions as conditional on this split. Repeating the entire 450 GPU-hour pipeline is not a prerequisite for an excellent master's thesis.

**Declared comparison asymmetry.** The game campaign fixes Grad-CAM, Grad-CAM++, and LayerCAM to the last convolutional block, while RG receives a large search. Elsewhere, the thesis explicitly observes that attribution performance depends heavily on layer selection, and the older split campaign includes layer-optimized baselines ([fixed CAM settings](../../tex/chapters/10_appendix.tex#L255); [layer dependence](../../tex/chapters/08_experiments.tex#L29)). Equal numbers of configurations would not automatically make the comparison fair, but an important baseline degree of freedom is being suppressed.

For the CNN localization claim, justify the fixed-layer convention and limit the conclusion to that convention, or add a small validation-only layer sweep for the strongest CAM competitors, followed by one locked test evaluation. This is a targeted check, not a demand for every possible implementation and hyperparameter. The transformer comparison has its own baseline grids and should not be dismissed because of this CNN-specific issue.

## Several interpretations should be narrowed

**Confirmed inferential overreach.** Frozen-setting transfer to VOC is described as testing whether the modes capture “model-specific rather than dataset-specific structure.” A comparison using the same ViT checkpoint on a second object-localization dataset establishes cross-dataset portability of the selected settings; it does not identify why portability occurs. Shared foreground-mask objectives or broadly useful sharpness settings remain alternative explanations. Replace the mechanistic interpretation with the observed transfer result. Identifying model specificity would require a different design and can remain future work ([transfer interpretation](../../tex/chapters/08_experiments.tex#L145)).

Similarly, calling high-temperature maps “perception-optimal” goes beyond the reported quantitative and qualitative comparison. The six qualitative examples are transparently identified, including five validation images, but they are illustrations rather than a perceptual evaluation. Describe the maps as broader or visually informative examples; reserve claims about perceptual optimality for evidence addressing that question ([temperature discussion](../../tex/chapters/08_experiments.tex#L135); [example provenance](../../tex/chapters/10_appendix.tex#L607)).

The thesis correctly acknowledges that downstream model improvement is untested. Preserve that distinction in its final answers to the research questions: the experiments establish properties of attribution procedures, not gains from correcting or controlling models. Likewise, the older split tables contain point estimates, including the small ResNet-18 Pointing Game difference of 0.972 versus 0.971. That is a reported advantage on this sample, not by itself compelling evidence of a stable superiority claim ([split results](../../tex/chapters/08_experiments.tex#L40); [limitations](../../tex/chapters/09_conclusion.tex#L29)).

## Prioritized repairs and completion criteria

| Priority | Action | Completion criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Must correct | State the actual game cohort construction and eligibility filters. | The manuscript and released loader agree on the 752-to-565 flow, target definition, and split identifiers; the older campaign's provenance is separately established. |
| Must correct | Narrow model-specific and perception-optimal interpretations. | Every such conclusion states what the comparison measured; alternative mechanisms are not presented as identified facts. |
| Needed for a persuasive 1.0 case | Explain tuning asymmetries and selection uncertainty. | The thesis justifies the fixed CNN layer convention or supplies a bounded matched check, and reports selection stability or explicitly limits the parameter-selection claim. |
| Needed for a persuasive 1.0 case | End each research question with an evidence-bounded answer. | A concise mapping identifies supporting experiments, contradictory metrics, population and checkpoint scope, and what remains untested. |
| Optional strengthening | Broaden checkpoints, datasets, or downstream intervention studies. | Undertake these only to support broader claims; they are not required to validate the narrower contribution already demonstrated. |

This assessment is based on the current TeX, tables, and the cited dataset and selection implementation; it does not claim an independent model-evaluation rerun. The thesis has enough substantive research for an excellent master's contribution. The remaining task is to make the experimental reasoning as disciplined as the best parts of its protocol.
