# Mathematical rigor and scope of the thesis claims

This review assesses the mathematical argument in the current thesis sources, with spot checks of Chapters 2, 3, 4, 7, and 11, the formalization guide, and the coverage map. It applies the [master's assessment framework](assessment-criteria.md). The standard is a defensible, independent mathematical investigation appropriate to an excellent master's thesis; complete machine verification and a new theorem for every architectural variation are not requirements. The CVPR Highlight component and the preprint provide substantial research material, but their status does not settle the correctness of the integrated claims.

The thesis has the mathematical substance for an excellent assessment. Its main weakness is the remaining distance between carefully qualified technical results and some broader descriptions of what those results establish. I would want the bounded scope repairs below before treating the mathematical case for 1.0 as persuasive. This is a source review, not an exhaustive certification of every proof or a report of a completed fresh Lean audit.

## Substantial strengths

The decomposition argument has a clear invariant and a useful separation between local algebra and network composition. The ungated affine output is explicit in Chapter 3, and correctness does not rely on all logits being positive. The distinction between the convex DC split, the stopping split, and regularized splits prevents several tempting but incorrect transfers of properties between them. In particular, the CNN theorem correctly withholds convexity and coordinatewise monotonicity when winner-takes-it-all pooling or input-dependent recentering is used. These are substantive mathematical qualifications, not cosmetic caveats. See [Chapter 3, definitions and CNN theorem](../../tex/chapters/03_decompositions.tex#L26).

The treatment of differentiation is also stronger than a purely formal manipulation of backpropagation equations. Chapter 3 distinguishes derivatives of the original heads from differentiating through an input-dependent recentering operation, and distinguishes a selected rule at a tie from a classical derivative. Chapter 4 makes the strict-gate and unique-pool-winner conditions explicit. The smooth splitting theorem assumes locally absolutely continuous derivatives and integrable curvature; the repaired regularizer remark now supplies the missing condition rather than claiming that smooth strict convexity alone suffices. These corrections materially improve the work. See [recentring and derivative conventions](../../tex/chapters/03_decompositions.tex#L513), [smooth splitting](../../tex/chapters/02_foundations.tex#L601), and [regularizer connection](../../tex/chapters/03_decompositions.tex#L458).

Finally, the thesis now separates normalized trajectory probabilities from signed discounted occupation measures, and the attention recovery proof explicitly states its block-local, conditioned scope. The repository openly records partial coverage. Missing Lean coverage is therefore not evidence that the corresponding handwritten theorem is false or unproved. Conversely, the README correctly acknowledges that a global routing equilibrium and the older occupation-recovery construction still require a complete identification between their encodings. That honesty deserves credit. See [attention recovery](../../tex/chapters/07_transformers.tex#L486) and [formalization scope](../../formalization/README.md#L108).

## Main concerns and actionable repairs

### 1. Make the signed affine output bridge explicit

**Confirmed scope inconsistency; must correct.** Chapter 4 defines every activation, including the scalar output, by a ReLU, then calls that output a target logit. Its alpha-beta recovery theorem explicitly assumes a positive output, with a zero-output stopping case. Chapter 3, in contrast, uses an ungated affine target logit. This difference matters: for (f(x)=\operatorname{ReLU}(x)-2) at (x=1), the affine logit has derivative (1), while adding the Chapter 4 output ReLU produces derivative (0). The two conventions cannot be silently identified. See [game output definition](../../tex/chapters/04_game_theory.tex#L366) and [recovery theorem](../../tex/chapters/04_game_theory.tex#L1527).

This is not a claim that affine-output mathematics is absent everywhere: the appendix mentions it and `Thesis/AffineOutputBackward.lean` supplies affine composition results. The missing part is a clear connection in the mathematical narrative. Add the affine-head formula and its relation to the initial game state or occupation combination, or explicitly restrict the theorem's advertised application. Distinguish that composition result from a full signed-output routing equilibrium, which the formalization guide does not claim. **Done when:** a reader can derive the attribution for positive, zero, and negative affine logits from the stated conventions, without importing an unstated output gate. A three-case worked example or small implementation check is sufficient; a general new architecture theorem is unnecessary.

### 2. Complete the attention policy definitions at zero mass

**Confirmed endpoint specification gap; must correct.** The KL specialization allows arbitrary nonnegative (u) and displays the optimizer \(\bar\pi u/\sum\bar\pi u\). For (u\equiv0), this is (0/0), not a probability distribution. The later Value-side proposition correctly treats the zero *value* case, but the displayed attention transition policies and cancellations still need an explicit zero-mass convention. The general routing chapter has an absorption convention, so this should be a short integration repair, not a new research project. See [KL specialization](../../tex/chapters/07_transformers.tex#L329), [attention policies](../../tex/chapters/07_transformers.tex#L278), and [general absorption convention](../../tex/chapters/04_game_theory.tex#L1190).

State normalization only for positive total mass; explicitly inherit or define absorption when the total vanishes. Carry this case distinction through the product of Value-routing and Value-projection policies, so cancellation of an intermediate denominator is justified only on its positive support. **Done when:** an all-zero Value sign branch has a normalized completed transition law, a defined occupation contribution, and a proof consistent with the zero-value proposition.

### 3. Keep structural guarantees attached to the actual construction

**Confirmed overbroad summary; must correct.** The introduction says the stabilized construction transforms any pretrained ReLU network into two monotone convex subnetworks. The detailed CNN theorem expressly excludes winner-takes-it-all pooling and input-dependent recentering from that guarantee, and even gives the recentered pair \( (\operatorname{ReLU}(p-n),0) \), whose first component decreases in (n). The precise theorem is the stronger part of the thesis; the summary should follow it. See [introduction contribution](../../tex/chapters/01_introduction.tex#L40) and [CNN scope and counterexample](../../tex/chapters/03_decompositions.tex#L736).

Separate the mathematical DC representation, the stabilized computational representation, and the empirically used pooling variant. **Done when:** each headline guarantee names the variant for which it holds, and the experimental method description identifies that variant. This avoids using convexity of an underlying representation as an automatic explanation of a modified implementation's empirical behavior.

### 4. Reduce geometric interpretation to what the geometry proves

**Confirmed missing ingredients in the interpretation; needed for a persuasive 1.0 case.** The vertex-gradient theorem is a sound ambient support-function statement, and its proof usefully explains the restricted visibility of vertices after homogenizing biased networks. The following prose nevertheless treats the score gap \(\langle v-v',\xi\rangle\) as answering what *minimally different* perception changes the active prototype. A score gap alone is not an input-space distance: even the unconstrained distance to the associated hyperplane requires division by \(\|v-v'\|_*\) for a specified perturbation norm. Valid input splits, other competing prototypes, and image constraints also matter. See [prototype interpretation](../../tex/chapters/02_foundations.tex#L580).

Call these quantities directional score margins unless a constrained minimum-perturbation problem is defined and solved. Similarly, present nonnegativity as a structural reason to investigate stability and localization, rather than a proof of those empirical properties. **Done when:** the text distinguishes the proved support-function geometry from proposed semantic interpretations. An optional toy example comparing a margin with a true perturbation distance would strengthen the exposition; a new large counterfactual benchmark is not required.

### 5. Connect conditional theory to empirical claims without assuming the bridge

**Applicability limitation, not a disproved theorem; needed for a persuasive 1.0 case.** The randomization results clearly state a conditional alive-probability bound or a strict row-overlap bound. The routing proof even explains why continuous randomization alone does not imply strict contraction. Those are good qualifications. Statements that the theorems show drastic changes under randomization should retain them when interpreting real architectures. Likewise, the uncertainty-tilt result lowers an expected proxy score; it does not by itself prove lower variability of actual QK logits. See [randomization assumptions](../../tex/chapters/11_trajectory.tex#L928) and [uncertainty proxy](../../tex/chapters/07_transformers.tex#L584).

Add a short claim-to-evidence table identifying which assumptions are proved for the tested model, checked empirically, or remain analytical conditions. Narrow the uncertainty wording to the proxy unless a validation study supports the stronger interpretation. **Done when:** theorem applicability and observed benchmark behavior are traceable separately. Deriving every randomization hypothesis for a realistic transformer is optional further research, not a prerequisite imposed on this master's thesis.

## Overall assessment

The mathematical ambition and demonstrated technical work are unusually strong for a master's thesis. The remaining concerns are chiefly definitional completeness, integration between theoretical models and evaluated implementations, and interpretation of results beyond their hypotheses. They should not be dismissed as wording trivia: these boundaries determine what the experiments can legitimately test. Correcting them would make a more convincing case for an excellent thesis than adding more unconnected theorems or maximizing the number of Lean links. The requested repairs are finite and mostly expository; full formalization remains optional strengthening under the master's assessment standard.
